Open Word Study

Free tools for studying the words of the Bible in Greek and Hebrew. No account and no cost, built only from open data.

Try one: read John 1:14 with the Greek under each word, see σκηνόω, "to dwell", in its 5 New Testament places, or δικαιοσύνη, "righteousness", in Matthew.

Three ways to start

  1. From a verse. Open the Reader at John 3:16 and tap any word. From there you can see its cross references, compare translations, or color the grammar of the chapter.
  2. From a word. In Word Study, type an English word like "love" or a spelling like "agape", and see everywhere it appears and how it is translated. For Old Testament words such as "hesed", use Hebrew Word Study.
  3. From a person or place. In the Factbook, find Peter or Jerusalem and open any verse that names them.

All the tools

  • Interlinear ReaderRead the English with the Greek or Hebrew word under each word. Start here if you do not read the original languages.
  • Compare TranslationsThree open English translations of a New Testament verse, with differences traced back to the Greek.
  • Word StudyAny Greek word: where it appears, its forms, how open translations render it, and words translated the same way.
  • Hebrew Word StudyThe same for every word of the Old Testament. Start with חֶסֶד, "loving devotion".
  • Word WheelOne word, every occurrence, a ring per translation. Start with σκηνόω, "to dwell".
  • Passage WorkbenchAny New Testament chapter word by word: rare words, repeated words, and where Greek editions differ.
  • Passage CompanionRead any chapter of the Bible with the margin open: cross references readers found helpful and, in the New Testament, notes on hard phrases, people and places.
  • Grammar LensColor the commands, the participles, or one word through any chapter to see its shape: Greek in the New Testament, Hebrew in the Old.
  • Verse FinderVerses that meet several conditions at once: words, people, places and grammar.
  • FactbookPeople, places and things the New Testament names, with every mention.
