Freedom Bible review: Mark 1

Suggested corrections to the Japanese Freedom Bible (jpnm), measured against the Robinson-Pierpont Byzantine Greek that eBible.org names as its master text. These are the Mark 1 rows sent to eBible so far. Each card shows the Japanese, the steps behind the suggestion, and the Greek word by word, so anyone can check it.

How the rows were made

  1. A script lines up each verse of jpnm with the Robinson-Pierpont Greek, the World English Bible (WEB) and 口語訳 (1955).
  2. An AI model reads each verse and drafts a suggestion, with a reason that cites Strong's numbers.
  3. A script checks each claim against STEPBible's tagged Greek (TAGNT): are the cited words in the Byzantine verse, and do the two Greek texts differ there?
  4. A native Japanese reader goes through each row and keeps, changes or drops it. Only rows that have been through that review are shown, with the reviewer's note at the bottom of each card.

The first drafts were written by AI. Treat each row as a suggestion for the editor, not a final reading. Greek data: STEPBible TAGNT (CC BY 4.0).

Why most rows mention two Greek texts

Greek New Testament manuscripts differ by a few words in many verses. The Byzantine text follows the majority of later manuscripts; eBible.org says the Freedom Bible follows it. The critical text (Nestle-Aland, NA28) follows a smaller group of older manuscripts; 口語訳, 新改訳 and most modern Bibles follow it. Many rows here are places where the Japanese reads like the critical text instead of the Byzantine. The cards mark words found only in one text: a dashed green box is Byzantine only, a struck-through red box is critical text only. That marking comes from STEPBible's tagged Greek (TAGNT), not from the AI.
